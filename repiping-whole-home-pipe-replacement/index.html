Signs You Need It

Signs It's Time To Repipe

Repiping is a bigger decision than a single repair, and these are the signs that make it the right one, especially when more than one of them shows up in the same home. We'll walk through which of these apply to your situation before recommending anything this significant.

Recurring Leaks In Different Spots

Leaks appearing in different sections of the same pipe run usually mean the whole line is failing, not just one point.

Discolored Water Sitewide

Rust-colored water from multiple fixtures points to widespread internal pipe corrosion.

Polybutylene Pipe

Polybutylene, common in homes built through the 1990s, is prone to failure and often flagged during inspections.

Persistently Low Pressure

Galvanized pipe narrows from internal corrosion over decades, and repiping is often the only real fix.

What To Expect

The Assessment Process

Before recommending anything, we inspect accessible sections of your plumbing, check water pressure and color at multiple fixtures, and ask about your home's age and any past leak history. That assessment tells us honestly whether the whole house needs new pipe or just the worst-affected section. If a partial repipe genuinely solves the problem, we'll say so rather than defaulting to the larger, more expensive job, and if whole-house work really is the better long-term value, we'll explain exactly why before you commit to it.

FAQs

Repiping Questions

What homeowners ask before committing to a repipe, since it's a bigger investment than a single repair.

Do I need to move out during a repipe?

Usually not for a partial repipe. A full whole-home repipe may require staying elsewhere for a day or two depending on scope.

PEX or copper, which is better?

Both are code-compliant and durable; PEX costs less and installs faster, copper has a longer track record. We'll explain the trade-offs for your home.

How much wall damage should I expect?

Modern techniques minimize openings, and we patch access points as part of the job, though final paint and finish work is typically on you.

What happens to my existing fixtures during a repipe?

Fixtures themselves usually stay in place; only the supply lines feeding them are replaced, then reconnected and tested.
