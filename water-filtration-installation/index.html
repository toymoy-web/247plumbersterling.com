Who Benefits

Signs Filtration Would Help

Filtration needs differ depending on whether you're on municipal water or a private well, and recommending the same system for both would genuinely be the wrong advice for one of them. We start by asking which supply you're on before talking about specific equipment.

Chlorine Taste Or Smell

Common on municipal supplies like Loudoun Water, a carbon filter addresses this directly.

Sediment Or Cloudiness

More common on well water, a sediment pre-filter is often the first step.

Staining From Iron Or Manganese

Reddish or brownish staining on fixtures is a real, visible sign of dissolved minerals worth filtering.

Wanting Fewer Bottles

A whole-home or under-sink system can be a practical, cost-effective, ongoing alternative to bottled water.

What We Install

Systems Matched To Your Water

The right system depends on what's actually in your water, not a one-size-fits-all recommendation.

Whole-Home Carbon Filtration

Addresses chlorine taste and odor right at the point where water first enters the home.

Sediment Pre-Filtration

Especially relevant for well-supplied homes, protects downstream fixtures and appliances.

Under-Sink Reverse Osmosis

Targeted, high-quality drinking-water filtration installed right at the kitchen tap.

Iron & Manganese Filtration

For well water with visible mineral staining, a dedicated media filter.

What To Expect

A Filtration Assessment

We start by asking what's actually bothering you, taste, smell, staining, or cloudiness, since that points toward a specific type of filtration rather than a generic recommendation. For standard municipal water, that's most often a carbon filter addressing chlorine taste directly. For well water, sediment and mineral content usually come first. We'll explain the real trade-offs between a whole-home system and a targeted under-sink unit before recommending either, since the more expensive option isn't always the better fit for your actual concern.

Local Factor

Municipal Vs. Well Water Here

Most of our service area runs on Loudoun Water's municipal supply, sourced from the Potomac River, which is treated and safe but commonly carries a chlorine taste and moderate hardness. Great Falls is the exception, running almost entirely on private wells, where the concern shifts to sediment, iron, and other naturally occurring minerals rather than chlorine. We ask which supply you're on before recommending a system, since the right filter for one is often the wrong one for the other.

FAQs

Filtration Questions

Common questions about whole-home and under-sink filtration, and how it differs from softening.

Do I need a whole-home system or just under-sink?

Depends entirely on your goal, whole-home addresses every single tap and appliance, under-sink is a lower-cost option focused just on drinking water.

Will filtration fix hard water too?

Filtration and softening are genuinely different things, a water softener specifically targets hardness minerals; see our water softener service for that.

How often do filters need to be changed?

Varies by system and water source, typically every 3-12 months, we'll set expectations for your specific setup.

How do I know which filtration system is right for me?

Call and describe your water's taste, smell, or appearance, we'll recommend based on that and your water source.
