Common Problems

Signs Your Toilet Needs Attention

Most toilet problems are quick, inexpensive fixes once diagnosed correctly, though a few genuinely call for full replacement rather than another repair. Knowing which category yours falls into before we arrive helps set realistic expectations for the visit.

Running After Flushing

Almost always a worn flapper or fill valve, a fast, low-cost fix.

Rocking Or Loose Base

Can mean a worn wax ring or a cracked flange, worth fixing before it leaks onto the floor.

Weak Or Incomplete Flush

Often a clogged trapway or a failing flush valve, not necessarily a sign you need a full replacement.

Cracks In The Bowl Or Tank

A crack means replacement, not repair, before it fails completely.

What To Expect

A Toilet Service Visit

Most toilet calls resolve with a straightforward parts swap, a fill valve, flapper, or wax ring, and we always carry the common sizes and styles right on the truck.

For a full new installation, we always measure the rough-in distance and check the flange condition carefully before ordering, since assuming a standard fit on an older toilet sometimes leads to a wrong-size unit and a second trip. Either way, we test the flush, check for leaks at the base and supply line, and confirm everything is sealed properly before calling the job finished.

Local Factor

Why Rough-In Size Varies Across Sterling

Sterling's homes span several decades of construction, and older bathrooms sometimes have a non-standard rough-in distance that a generic replacement toilet won't fit without adjustment. We measure before ordering rather than assuming a standard 12-inch rough-in, which avoids a second trip for the wrong-size unit, especially common in Sterling's older-phase homes.

Caller Questions

Running, Leaking Or Overflowing Toilet?

A toilet that runs, hisses or refills on its own usually needs a new fill valve or flapper. Water around the base can mean a worn seal or a loose connection. A clog that will not clear may sit deeper in the line.

If a toilet overflows, close the shutoff valve behind it first. If other fixtures back up too, the problem is in the main line, so see our drain cleaning page.

Constant running

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve. A common, quick repair.

Water at the base

Often a failed seal. The floor should be checked for damage.

Gurgling or slow flush

May indicate a clog beyond the toilet.

Replace instead

Cracked bowls and repeated repairs favor a new toilet.

FAQs

Toilet Questions

What Sterling homeowners ask most about toilet repair and installation, answered plainly.

Can you match my existing toilet's color or style?

In most cases yes, standard colors are widely and readily available. We'll always confirm before ordering anything.

Is a running toilet actually wasting much water?

Yes, a running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day, it's worth fixing quickly even though it seems minor.

Do you install low-flow or water-efficient toilets?

Yes, and we can explain what actually flushes reliably versus what just claims to.

Do you offer any warranty on the work?

Workmanship is covered on every installation. We'll explain the specific terms before starting.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A flapper that no longer seals or a worn fill valve is the usual cause. Both are inexpensive parts, and a technician can replace them during one visit.

My toilet is leaking around the base. Is that serious?

It can be if it has gone on a while. A worn seal lets water reach the subfloor. Have it checked before the floor softens.

Should I repair or replace the toilet?

Repair suits a sound toilet with a single fault. Replace when the bowl is cracked or repairs repeat. The technician will explain either way.
