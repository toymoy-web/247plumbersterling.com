Signs You Need Us

Signs Your Water Heater Needs A Repair

A water heater rarely fails without warning. Most units show one of these signs weeks or months before they stop working entirely, and catching it early usually means a repair instead of an emergency replacement.

No Hot Water

Cold showers or a unit that can't keep up usually points to a failed heating element or thermostat, not always a full tank replacement.

Rusty Or Discolored Water

Rust-colored hot water often signals internal tank corrosion, worth checking before it fails completely.

Popping Or Rumbling Noises

Sediment buildup rumbling as the unit heats is common in older tanks and speeds up wear if left alone.

Leaking Around The Base

Moisture at the base can mean a loose fitting or a failing tank, and the difference changes whether repair or replacement makes sense.

Local Factor

Why Sterling Water Heaters Wear Out Faster

Sterling draws its water through Loudoun Water, sourced from the Potomac River, and homes on this supply commonly deal with moderately hard water, meaning dissolved calcium and magnesium that accumulate inside a water heater tank over time. That mineral buildup is a genuine, well-documented reason tanks in this region often show sediment-related symptoms, rumbling, slower recovery, reduced capacity, years before a tank's rated lifespan ends.

A periodic tank flush is the practical response, and it's worth asking about at your next service call rather than waiting for a full failure.

As a rough guide, an annual flush is a reasonable starting point for most households on this water supply, though a household running an unusually large amount of hot water, a big family, a home gym with a steam shower, may benefit from flushing more often.

Caller Questions

Leaking Water Heater Or No Hot Water Tonight?

Water at the base of the tank, rumbling noises or lukewarm showers are the usual reasons for a call. A small drip from a fitting is often repairable. Water coming from the tank body usually means replacement.

Tank age matters. An older corroded tank rarely justifies a repair bill, and we say so plainly. If it makes sense to replace, our water heater replacement service covers the next step.

Stop The Damage

Tank Leaking Right Now

Shut the cold supply

Close the valve on the cold water line above the tank to stop refilling.

Cut power or gas

Turn the electric breaker off or set the gas control to off or pilot.

Contain the water

Use towels or a wet vacuum and keep the area clear of electronics.

Call us

Use the 24/7 line if the leak is active.

FAQs

Common Questions

The questions we hear most from Sterling homeowners calling about their water heater. If yours isn't listed here, ask when you call, our dispatchers can usually answer on the spot.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace?

Depends on tank age and the part needed. We give you both numbers before you decide, no pressure either way.

How fast can you get here for no hot water?

Our emergency line is answered 24/7, and most calls before 2pm get a same-day window.

Do you service tankless units?

Yes, tankless diagnosis and repair is available alongside standard tank-style service.

My water heater is leaking. Can it be repaired?

It depends on where the water comes from. A leaking valve or fitting can often be repaired. A leak from the tank itself generally means the tank has failed.

There is no hot water at all. What should I check first?

Check the breaker or the gas pilot and look for water around the tank. If neither explains it, call us and we will diagnose the heating element, thermostat or burner.

Repair or replace an older tank?

If the tank is old and corroded, replacement usually costs less than repeated repairs. The technician gives an honest view after inspecting it.

Do you work on tankless units?

Yes, see our tankless water heater service page for flushing and repair.
