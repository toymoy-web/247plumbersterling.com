Signs Of A Problem

Signs Your Basement Needs Waterproofing

A sump pump alone doesn't always solve the underlying water intrusion problem, especially when water is entering along a foundation wall rather than just rising up through the floor. Identifying the actual entry point is the difference between a lasting fix and a pump that just keeps working harder.

Water Along The Foundation Wall

Water entering along a wall, not just from the floor, points to a foundation or grading issue a sump pump alone won't fix.

Musty Smell Or Visible Mold

Persistent moisture in a basement creates conditions mold needs to grow.

Efflorescence On Concrete

White, chalky mineral deposits on basement walls are a sign of water moving through the concrete over time.

Sump Pump Running Constantly

A pump that never stops cycling suggests more water is getting in than a pump alone should have to handle.

What To Expect

A Waterproofing Assessment

We start by identifying where water is actually entering, along a wall, through the floor, or around a specific penetration, since each points to a different solution. An interior French drain channels water along the foundation's perimeter to a properly sized sump pump, addressing the entry point rather than just pumping out what's already inside. We'll walk the basement with you, point out what we're seeing, and explain honestly whether a full system is warranted or whether a smaller, targeted fix genuinely solves your specific situation.

Safety First

What To Do Before We Arrive

Avoid Standing Water Near Outlets

Don't enter a flooded area of the basement near an electrical outlet, panel, or plugged-in appliance.

Shut Off Power If Reachable Safely

If the breaker for the basement is accessible without stepping through water, shut it off first.

Move Stored Items Off The Floor

If it's safe to do so, lift boxes and belongings off wet flooring to limit further damage.

Interior drainage and sump pump work is installed to Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards for drainage and discharge, the same code our technicians follow on every job.

Being Honest About Scope

What We Handle Vs. What We Don't

Interior drainage, sump pump systems, and vapor barriers are plumbing work we handle directly. Exterior grading changes, gutter and downspout extensions, and structural foundation repair fall outside plumbing, and we'll say so plainly rather than taking on work outside our scope, pointing you to the right specialist when needed.

Local Factor

Which Sterling Homes See This Most

Sterling's basement-equipped homes, mostly the mid-century colonials built with full basements rather than the newer slab-on-grade townhomes, are where we see the most water intrusion calls, especially during heavy rain events. If your home sits on lower ground or has taken on water before, that history is worth mentioning when you call, since it changes how aggressively we recommend addressing it.

FAQs

Waterproofing Questions

Common questions about basement water intrusion and drainage, since it's often confused with a simple sump pump fix.

Will a sump pump alone fix my basement?

Sometimes, but if water is entering along walls rather than just rising from the floor, a drainage system addresses the actual entry point better.

Can you fix the exterior grading too?

We can flag grading issues, but the regrading work itself is typically done by a landscaping or excavation contractor, which we can help you coordinate with.

How long does interior drain installation take?

Most interior French drain systems complete in one to two days depending on basement size.

Will waterproofing work require me to leave the house?

No, work is contained to the basement and doesn't require you to vacate the home.
