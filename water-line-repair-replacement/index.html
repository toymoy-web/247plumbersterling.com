Signs Of A Problem

Signs Your Main Water Line Is Failing

The main line from the street to your home is easy to overlook until it fails, since it runs underground and out of sight for its entire life until something goes wrong. These are the signs that tell us to start looking there rather than somewhere inside the house.

Sudden Low Pressure Throughout The House

Pressure loss affecting every fixture at once, not just one, often traces back to the main line.

Wet Spot In The Yard

An unexplained soggy patch, especially in cold weather when everything else is dry, is a classic main line leak sign.

Unusually High Water Bill

A main line leak runs continuously and shows up fast on your usage.

Discolored Water After Utility Work

Sediment stirred up by nearby utility work can mean a line disturbance worth checking.

Local Factor

Caller Questions

Leak Between The Meter And The House?

The service line runs from the meter to your home and is easy to forget. Signs include a meter that moves with the house shut off, low pressure, or a wet or sunken patch in the yard.

Repair or replace depends on pipe material, how many failures there are, and how deep the line runs. We explain both paths before work begins, including trenchless replacement where the site allows it.

Yard clues

Soft ground, a bright green strip of grass or pooling water along the line suggests a buried leak.

Meter clue

Turn everything off and watch the meter. Movement means water is leaving somewhere.

Older pipe

Homes with older service lines may have lead or aging steel. Ask the dispatcher about material before the visit.

Pressure drop

Weak pressure at every tap can point to a failing main line rather than a single fixture.

FAQs

Water Line Questions

Common questions about main water line repair and replacement, including who's actually responsible for it.

Will my yard be torn up?

Not necessarily, trenchless methods often replace the line with just a small access pit at each end.

Who's responsible, me or the utility?

Typically the homeowner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house. The utility owns the line up to the meter. We'll help clarify for your specific situation.

How long until I have water again?

Most trenchless replacements restore water service the same day. Traditional dig repairs can take a day or two depending on scope.

Do you coordinate with my HOA if one exists?

Yes, if your community requires notice or approval for exterior work, we can help provide the documentation needed.

How do I know the leak is in the line from the meter?

Shut off the main valve at the house and check the meter. If it still moves, the leak sits between the meter and your shutoff. A leak detection visit can confirm the exact section.

Do I have to dig up the whole yard?

Not always. Locating the leak first limits digging, and trenchless replacement may fit some lines. We go over the options before work starts.

Repair the leak or replace the whole line?

A single clean break may only need a section repair. A line with repeated failures or old material is usually better replaced. The quote covers the path we recommend and why.

Who handles the part near the meter?

The utility generally owns the meter and its connection. We handle your side of it. Ask the dispatcher if you are unsure where the line of responsibility sits.
