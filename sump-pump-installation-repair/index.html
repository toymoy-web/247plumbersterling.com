Who Needs This

Which Sterling Homes Actually Need A Sump Pump

Not every home needs the same setup, and an oversized system is money spent on protection you don't need. Here's how we think about it.

Basement-Level Colonials

Sterling's mid-century colonial-style homes with full finished basements are the housing type where a failed pump does the most real damage.

Homes On Low-Lying Grading

Yards that slope toward the foundation instead of away from it push more water toward the basement during storms.

Homes That Have Flooded Before

A basement that's taken on water once will do it again without a properly sized pump in place.

Homes With An Aging Pump

A pump that's silently near the end of its life is the one that fails during the storm that actually needed it.

Our Approach

Installation & Repair Done Right

A sump pump is only as good as the system around it, sizing, discharge routing, and backup power all matter as much as the pump itself. Here's what we check on every visit before recommending anything.

Correctly Sized For Your Basement

An undersized pump is a common, avoidable reason a "working" pump still floods during heavy rain.

Battery Backup Options

A pump that only runs on house power fails during the exact power outage a big storm often causes.

Discharge Routed Away From The Foundation

A discharge line that dumps water too close to the house just recycles the same problem right back toward your foundation, and proper routing is part of meeting Virginia's plumbing code (IPC) requirements for sump discharge.

Tested Under Real Load Before We Leave

We run water through the system to confirm it actually cycles correctly under real conditions, not just that it powers on.

Sump Pump Repair

Repairs We Handle On Existing Pumps

Many sump pump problems are fixable without a full replacement. When a pump stops, runs constantly, or cycles on and off, we diagnose the specific failure first and tell you honestly whether a repair or a new unit makes more sense.

Stuck Or Failed Float Switch

A float that sticks against the pit wall or fails electrically either never starts the pump or never stops it. This is one of the most common repairs and often a quick one.

Failed Check Valve

A bad check valve lets water drain back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump short-cycles and wears out early. Replacing the valve is inexpensive and extends the life of the pump.

Clogged Intake Or Impeller

Silt, gravel, or debris can block the intake screen or jam the impeller, leaving a pump that hums but moves little water. Clearing and testing it restores flow when the motor is still healthy.

Blocked Or Frozen Discharge Line

If the discharge line is crushed, clogged, or frozen, the pump runs without moving water out. We trace the line, clear the blockage, and confirm it exits well away from the foundation.

Power And Switch Problems

A tripped GFCI outlet, a dead battery backup, or a failed cord can look like a dead pump. We check the electrical path before condemning the pump itself.

Worn-Out Motor

A pump that is many years old, noisy, and cycling constantly is usually near the end of its life. When repair would cost more than it is worth, we say so and quote a correctly sized replacement.

FAQs

Sump Pump Questions

Sizing and backup power come up on nearly every sump pump call, so we've answered them upfront, along with a few things worth knowing before we arrive.

How do I know if my current pump is undersized?

If your basement still takes on water during heavy rain with the pump running, sizing is the first thing worth checking.

Is a battery backup really necessary?

If your basement has flooded before, or the pump matters most during the exact storms that also cause power outages, yes, it's worth the added cost.

How often should a sump pump be tested?

A quick test before storm season, and again mid-winter, catches most failures before they matter.

Does my location in Sterling affect what I need?

Yes, homes closer to the floodplain edge generally benefit more from a battery backup than homes on higher ground, and we'll factor that into what we recommend.

Why does my sump pump run all the time?

A stuck float, a failed check valve or high groundwater can cause it.

Should I install a backup pump?

A backup helps during outages or heavy rain. The technician can advise.

How long does a sump pump last?

Lifespan varies with use and quality. Testing before storm season helps.
