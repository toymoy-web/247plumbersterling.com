Why It Matters

What Backflow Prevention Actually Does

A backflow preventer stops contaminated water from being pulled backward into the clean municipal supply, a real public health safeguard, not a bureaucratic formality. Skipping required testing doesn't just risk a compliance issue, it risks the actual failure mode the device exists to prevent.

Irrigation System Protection

Lawn irrigation systems are a common backflow risk point requiring a dedicated, properly tested device year-round.

Required For Many Properties

Many jurisdictions and water utilities require annual testing on properties with an irrigation system or certain commercial connections, and missing a cycle can trigger a compliance notice.

Failed Devices Go Unnoticed

A failed backflow preventer often shows no obvious symptoms until tested.

Commercial Requirements

Many commercial properties have backflow testing requirements tied to their water service.

What To Expect

A Backflow Testing Visit

Testing itself is quick, a certified tester checks the device under controlled conditions to confirm it's genuinely preventing backflow correctly, and we document the results in the format your water utility or jurisdiction requires. If a device fails testing, we'll explain what repair is needed and whether it's a simple seal or valve replacement versus a full unit swap. For new irrigation systems or commercial connections needing a device installed for the first time, we size and install it to meet your local requirements from day one.

Local Factor

Testing Requirements Around Here

Backflow devices are tested and installed to the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standard and, where applicable, ASSE-certified device requirements set by the manufacturer.

Many of Sterling's newer subdivisions include lawn irrigation systems, which is exactly the kind of connection that typically requires an annual backflow test under local water utility rules. If you're not sure whether your property is on a required testing schedule, we can check your device and confirm before your utility flags it.

Pricing

Testing alone is inexpensive; repairs or new installations cost more depending on the device and whether it's a residential or commercial-grade unit. Most residential irrigation testing falls at the lower end of the range, while larger commercial devices or a full new installation naturally push toward the higher end.

FAQs

Backflow Questions

Common questions about backflow testing requirements and service, especially for irrigation-equipped homes.

Do I actually need this if I have an irrigation system?

In most cases yes, irrigation systems are one of the most common backflow risk points requiring an annual test.

How do I know if my device already failed?

Testing is the only reliable way, a failed device often shows no visible signs.

Will you handle the paperwork with my utility?

Yes, we provide documentation in the format needed to satisfy local testing requirements.

What happens if my property fails a backflow test?

We'll explain the needed repair, complete it, and retest to confirm compliance before finalizing documentation.
