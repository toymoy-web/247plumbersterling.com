Common Plumbing Repairs In Sterling, VA

Most plumbing calls fall into a small number of recurring categories, and knowing which one you're dealing with helps set expectations before we even arrive. Here's what we see most often across Sterling homes, and roughly what each one usually means.

Leaky Faucets & Fixtures

A slow drip wastes real water over a year and usually points to a worn washer, cartridge, or seal we can replace same visit.

Running Or Phantom-Flushing Toilets

A toilet that runs after flushing or refills on its own is almost always a fill valve or flapper, a fast, low-cost fix.

Low Water Pressure

Pressure that's dropped gradually across the whole house often traces back to aging galvanized supply lines, common in Sterling's older-phase construction.

Slab & Wall Leaks

An unexplained wet spot on a slab or a rising water bill with no visible leak calls for electronic leak detection before anyone opens a wall or floor.

Local Factor

Why Repair Approach Depends On Your Home's Age

Sterling was built out in phases across several decades, and that matters for how a repair actually goes. Older sections still run galvanized supply lines and older-style fixtures, which sometimes need a specific part sourced rather than pulled straight off the truck. Newer townhomes and post-2000 builds use PEX supply lines and modern fixtures, which usually means a faster, more straightforward repair. Telling us roughly when your home was built helps us bring the right parts on the first visit instead of a second trip.

Repair Or Replace

When A Repair Is (And Isn't) Enough

Not every plumbing problem justifies replacing the whole fixture or run of pipe, but some genuinely do. We'll always tell you honestly which situation you're in rather than defaulting to the bigger job. A repair done right on a healthy fixture can last another decade; the same repair on a fixture already past its useful life is money that would have gone further toward replacement.

Usually Just A Repair

  • One fixture affected, rest of the house is fine
  • Problem is new, not recurring
  • Pipe or fixture is under 10-15 years old
  • No visible corrosion at the connection

Worth Discussing Replacement

  • Same repair keeps coming back
  • Visible corrosion or mineral scale at the fitting
  • Fixture or pipe run predates a major renovation
  • Multiple fixtures on the same line are affected

Repair Questions From Sterling Homeowners

A few things we hear on almost every call, answered plainly before you have to ask.

Do you charge a fee just to come look at it?

We quote the visit and diagnosis upfront when you call, so there's no surprise trip charge.

Can you match parts to an older fixture?

In most cases yes. If an exact match isn't available, we'll explain the closest option before doing any work.

Is a same-day repair actually possible?

For most single-fixture repairs, yes, our trucks carry the common parts needed to finish in one visit.

We'll tell you honestly whether a spot repair makes sense or whether that section is close to needing a broader repipe conversation.
