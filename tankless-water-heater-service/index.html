Signs Of Trouble

Signs Your Tankless Unit Needs Service

Tankless units fail differently than tank-style heaters, usually a specific error code or a gradual drop in flow rather than a sudden total failure. Catching these early usually means a straightforward service call instead of an emergency replacement down the road.

Fluctuating Water Temperature

Temperature that swings hot and cold mid-shower often points to a flow sensor or scale buildup issue.

Error Codes On The Display

Modern tankless units display a specific error code, which tells us exactly where to start diagnosing.

Reduced Flow Rate

Mineral scale buildup inside the heat exchanger narrows flow over time, especially on hard water.

Unit Won't Ignite

A gas or ignition fault stops the unit from firing at all, usually a fixable component-level repair.

What To Expect

A Tankless Service Visit

We start by asking what you're seeing, an error code, a temperature swing, reduced flow, since tankless units are diagnostic-friendly in a way tank heaters aren't. Most units display a specific fault code that tells us where to look before we even open the unit. A descaling visit involves flushing the heat exchanger with a cleaning solution to dissolve mineral buildup, restoring flow and efficiency. A component repair means testing the flow sensor, ignition system, and heat exchanger individually to find the actual failure point rather than replacing parts by guesswork.

Local Factor

Why Descaling Matters More Here

Homes on Loudoun Water's supply, drawn from the Potomac River, commonly deal with moderately hard water, and a tankless unit's narrow heat exchanger passages are more sensitive to mineral scale than a tank's wide interior. An annual descaling flush is the single best thing you can do to keep a tankless unit running at its rated efficiency and lifespan on this water supply, and it's a service we can schedule on a recurring basis.

FAQs

Tankless Questions

Common questions from Sterling homeowners considering or already running a tankless unit, answered plainly.

How often should a tankless unit be descaled?

Annually is a reasonable standard on this water supply, more often for larger households running more hot water.

Is tankless worth it over a traditional tank?

For many households yes, especially with consistent high hot-water demand, but it depends on your specific usage and budget.

Can you repair the brand I have?

Yes, we service all major tankless brands, not just the ones we install ourselves.

Can tankless units run out of hot water?

Not in the traditional sense, but flow rate limits mean running multiple high-demand fixtures at once can still stretch capacity.
