Local Background

Sterling Park, Sterling, Loudoun County

Sterling Park is the planned residential section of Sterling that sits east of Route 28. It was built on about 1,762 acres of former farmland that the Broyhill family bought in 1961. The developers had to build their own through road, which is now Sterling Boulevard, to link the community to Route 7.

Sterling Park is not a separate town. It is part of Sterling, so the mailing address is simply Sterling, VA. The area is described as one of the first planned communities in eastern Loudoun County, and its first homes date to the 1960s.

That age is the useful fact for a homeowner. Homes that are more than 55 years old have usually had parts of their plumbing replaced at some point, but nobody can tell from the street which parts. That is why we look at the actual house before recommending anything.

Plumbing Guidance

What Is Known, What Is General, What Is Not Known

Known About The Area

Sterling Park was developed starting in 1961 and built out through the 1960s. Park View High School opened there in 1976. Those are documented facts about the community, not about any one house.

General Plumbing Fact

Any home of this age can have a mix of original and replaced plumbing. Supply lines, drain lines, fixtures and water heaters wear out on different schedules, and renovation history changes the picture house by house.

Not Known Until We Look

We do not assume which pipe material your house has. The material depends on when the house was built, what the previous owners changed and what repairs were done. We confirm it on site and tell you what we find.

If you are comparing quotes, ask each plumber what they found in your house rather than what they expect to find in the neighborhood.

If Water Is Running

What To Do Before We Arrive

Stop The Water

Turn the main shutoff fully off. In many homes it is near where the line enters the house or near the water heater.

Protect What You Can

Move rugs and electronics away from the wet area and photograph the damage for your records.

Keep Power Safe

If water is near outlets or the panel, leave the breaker alone unless you can reach it safely and dry.
