Plumbing In Great Falls, Virginia

Great Falls is genuinely different from the rest of our service area. Most homes here sit on multi-acre lots without municipal water or sewer, running instead on private wells and septic systems, which changes what "plumbing problem" even means. The area takes its name from Great Falls Park itself, the dramatic set of rapids and waterfalls along the Potomac River, and the surrounding land, much of it near Riverbend Park and the historic Great Falls Village, has stayed low-density and rural in character even as the rest of Fairfax County has built up around it. That rural character is exactly why well and septic systems are the norm here rather than the exception.

Local Plumbing Challenges

Why Great Falls Is A Different Kind Of Call

Every one of these issues traces back to the same root cause, no municipal utility connection, so we approach every Great Falls call with that context already in mind.

Private Well Systems

Without a municipal water connection, a failed well pump isn't an inconvenience, it's a total loss of water to the house, and we prioritize these calls accordingly.

Well Water Quality

Well water commonly carries more iron, sediment, or sulfur than treated municipal water, which affects fixture staining, water heater buildup, and whether a whole-house filtration system makes sense.

Septic-Adjacent Plumbing

Interior drain and sewer work on a septic-connected home has to account for the septic system's own capacity, not just the pipe itself.

Large Estate Homes

Multi-bathroom, multi-acre properties, some with pools, mean higher-capacity water heating and pressure systems than a typical subdivision home needs.

Standards

Code Compliance On Well & Septic Properties

Private wells and septic systems in Virginia are regulated separately by the Virginia Department of Health, not the plumbing code, but the interior plumbing connecting to them still has to meet the same Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standard we follow on every job. When a well or septic issue is involved, we're upfront about which part of the system is ours to fix and which needs a specialized well or septic contractor.

Safety First

What To Do If You Notice These Well Or Septic Signs

Discolored Or Odd-Tasting Water

Stop using the water for drinking or cooking and call, this can signal a contamination issue worth checking before anything else.

Sewage Odor Near The Drain Field

A persistent smell near your septic drain field is an early warning sign of a failing system, worth addressing before it becomes a backup.

Gurgling Drains Throughout The House

Multiple drains gurgling at once, not just one, often points to a septic-side issue rather than a simple clog.

No Water At All

Check your well pump's breaker first, a tripped breaker is a common, easy fix before assuming pump failure.
